Bridging Citizen Science and Mark-Recapture: An iNaturalist-to-Wildbook Integration Tool

We have developed an automated pipeline that seamlessly integrates iNaturalist observations into Wildbook's bulk import system, enabling researchers to leverage the vast repository of citizen science data for mark-recapture studies. This Python-based tool queries the iNaturalist API for recent observations of target species, automatically downloads associated photographs and metadata, and formats everything into Wildbook's standardized CSV structure. By bridging these two platforms, researchers can now rapidly incorporate high-quality wildlife sightings from a global community of naturalists into their existing photo-identification workflows.

The tool includes intelligent filtering mechanisms designed specifically for mark-recapture applications. It automatically excludes captive or cultivated specimens, ensuring only wild population data enters your database. An interactive HTML review interface allows researchers to manually curate observations before import, with smart auto-filtering that deselects observations lacking open licenses, those flagged as non-organism evidence (tracks, scat, molts), skull and bone specimens from museum collections, and observations requiring identification confirmation. This quality control step is critical for maintaining the integrity of photo-ID databases where individual recognition depends on clear, properly licensed images of live animals.

For social species where multiple individuals may appear in a single observation, the tool offers a unique splitting and merging capability. Multi-photo observations can be automatically divided into separate Wildbook encounters (one per photo) while maintaining their relationship through a shared Sighting ID—essential for species like jaguars or sea turtles where aggregations occur but individual tracking remains the priority. Researchers retain full control through the HTML interface, with the ability to merge split observations back together when all photos clearly depict the same individual. The system also handles technical challenges like converting animated GIFs to static JPEG frames, ensuring compatibility with computer vision identification algorithms.

Geographic precision is maintained through intelligent location parsing, where the tool maps iNaturalist's verbatim locality descriptions to standardized location IDs. For instance, observations from "Poconé, BR-MT" are automatically tagged with "Mato Grosso" as the location identifier, while preserving exact GPS coordinates and original locality strings for spatial analysis. All observations are tagged as "unapproved" upon import, allowing research teams to apply their own verification protocols before incorporating encounters into population estimates. By automating the tedious work of data acquisition, format conversion, and preliminary quality control, this tool allows biologists to focus on what matters most: analyzing population trends, identifying individuals, and advancing conservation outcomes through scientifically rigorous mark-recapture studies.
